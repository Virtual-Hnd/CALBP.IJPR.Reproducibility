Scholl vs Otto — HV Distribution

Interaction α × γ → Normalized HV

Each line represents a fixed cobot speed (α). The x-axis varies the sequential overhead (γ). Parallel lines = no interaction (α and γ act independently). Crossing lines = significant interaction (the best α depends on the γ level). For example, a fast cobot (low α) may excel when coordination is cheap (low γ), but lose its advantage when sequential overhead is high.

S/N ratio (larger-is-better)

Taguchi Signal-to-Noise ratio: S/N = −10·log₁₀(mean(1/y²)). Higher S/N (in dB) = the factor level produces both a high response and low variability across instances. The factor whose S/N curve has the steepest slope is the most influential. A flat curve means the factor has little impact on the response. Optimal setting = the level with the highest S/N for each factor.

Design of Experiments — Taguchi L9

Principle

We study how 4 human-robot collaboration factors affect the quality of bi-objective CALBP solutions (cost vs energy).

Instead of testing all 34=81 combinations, the Taguchi L9 orthogonal array reduces to 9 configurations while capturing the main effects of each factor.

Each config is crossed with 28 benchmark instances → 252 runs in total.

Solver: ε-constraint MILP (Audrey competitive, CPLEX 22.1.1, 300 s / iteration)

ε-constraint Method

Step 1: Solve min Cost (no energy constraint) → point (Cmax, Emin)
Step 2: Add constraint Cost ≤ Cmax−1 and re-solve.
Repeat by decreasing the cost bound at each iteration until infeasibility.
→ Each iteration yields one point of the Pareto front.

Pareto Front Quality Indicators

To evaluate the quality of a Pareto front, we use 5 complementary indicators (taxonomy from Zitzler et al. 2003). The reference point is computed as r = (max C, max E) × 1.10 and the ideal point z* = (min C, min E).

Normalized HV
Hypervolume / total area
Measures the area dominated by the front, normalized by the rectangle [z*, r].

Best → 1 (the front fills the entire space).
Captures: convergence + coverage
Ref: Zitzler & Thiele (1999)
Spacing (SP)
Std. deviation of nearest-neighbour L1 distances.
Measures whether points are uniformly spread.

Best → 0 (perfect spacing).
Captures: distribution uniformity
Ref: Schott (1995)
Spread (Δ)
Uniformity + extent combined.
Includes distances to the bounding-box boundary solutions.

Best → 0 (ideal distribution).
Captures: uniformity + extreme coverage
Ref: Deb et al. (2002)
Max Spread (MS)
Bounding box diagonal of the front in objective space.
Measures the range of proposed trade-offs.

Best → max (more diversity).
Captures: trade-off extent
Ref: Zitzler et al. (2000)
Optimality Rate
% of certified optimal points by CPLEX (MIP gap = 0%).
100% = every point is an exact solution.

Best → 100%
Captures: reliability / certification
Time limit: 300 s

Dashboard Reading Guide

"Overview" Tab

  • Progress grid: green cell = solved, red = error, grey = pending. The number in each cell = number of Pareto points.
  • Pareto front: select an instance to see all 9 fronts overlaid (1 per config).
  • Mode distribution: stacked bars showing what % of time is allocated to each collaboration mode.

"Pareto Indicators" Tab

  • HV Heatmap: quality overview by instance × config. Darker colors = higher HV = better front.
  • Indicator correlation: Spacing vs HV, colored by |PF|. Shows whether more points → better front.

"Config Analysis" Tab

  • Radar: normalized profile of each config on the 5 indicators. Larger polygon = better.
  • HV/CPU Boxplots: variability of each config across all instances.

"DOE Analysis" Tab

  • Main effects: how each factor (α, β, γ, σ) affects the chosen response. Upward curve = factor increases the response.
  • Relative importance: effect range of each factor. The largest = the most influential factor.
  • S/N ratio: Taguchi signal-to-noise ratio (−10·log₁₀(mean(1/y²))). Higher S/N = the factor level produces a high response with low variability. Steepest curve = most influential factor. Pick the level with the highest S/N for each factor.
  • Interaction α×γ: each line is a fixed α (cobot speed), x-axis = γ (sequential overhead). Parallel lines = no interaction. Crossing/diverging lines = the optimal cobot speed depends on coordination cost — a key insight for line design.
